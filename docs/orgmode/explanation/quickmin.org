#+TITLE: Quick-min
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil num:nil

Quick-min keeps a velocity and damps it by a projection before an
explicit Euler step. Write ~F = -∇f~ and ~Δt~ for the time step. The
reference update is

: v ← (v · F / |F|²) F    when v · F ≥ 0 and |F| > 0
: v ← 0                   otherwise
: v ← v + Δt F
: Δx = Δt v

~quickmin_baseline~ is that update and nothing else. A negative
projection takes the second branch, so the velocity that enters the
Euler kick has no component opposing ~F~. A zero force has no
direction and takes the same branch.

The session adds three pieces that the other first-order methods
already own.

The displacement cap is ~Solver::set_atom_maxmove~ or
~Control::maxmove~. When it shortens ~Δx~, the velocity is multiplied
by ~|Δx taken| / |Δx proposed|~. That is the ratio FIRE uses after its
own cap. The next projection then sees the motion that happened. On

: E = ½ (y - 0.2 x)² + 0.02 x²

from ~(2, 0)~ with ~Δt = 0.2~ and cap ~0.08~, eighty reference steps
that keep the uncapped velocity stay above ~10⁻⁷~, and the same steps
with the rescale fall below ~10⁻⁸~.

The time step uses FIRE's factors: ~n_min = 5~, ~f_inc = 1.1~,
~f_dec = 0.5~, ~Δt_max = 2.5 Δt₀~. More than ~n_min~ steps with
~F · v > 0~ grow ~Δt~. ~F · v < 0~ shrinks it, down to ~10⁻¹²~; the
velocity on that branch is already zero from the reference rule.
~F · v = 0~ leaves ~Δt~ alone. A resting velocity is not an uphill
step, and halving the caller's ~Δt~ before any motion only slows the
opening. On ~E = ½ x²~ from ~x = 1~ with ~Δt₀ = 0.05~, the growing
step reaches ~|x| < 10⁻³~ in fewer iterations than the fixed step.
~Solver::set_quickmin_adapt(false)~ selects the fixed reference step.

A cell block is an index ~at~. Coordinates ~[0, at)~ and ~[at, n)~
each take the reference replacement on their own force, then the Euler
kick uses the shared ~Δt~. A cell velocity that opposes the stress is
zeroed even when the atomic alignment keeps ~v · F~ positive for the
whole vector. One joint projection would replace both blocks by a
single component along the joint force and spend atomic momentum on
the opposing cell direction. On ~E = ½ (x² + c²)~ at ~(-1, 1)~ with
~v = (3, 1)~, ~F = (1, -1)~ and ~Δt = 0.1~, the split step reaches a
lower value than the joint projection, and the cell velocity after the
replacement is only the Euler kick ~Δt F_c~.
~Solver::set_quickmin_cell~ sets the index. The C entry is
~RGMIN_QUICKMIN~ and ~rgmin_solver_set_quickmin_cell~ (ABI minor 29).

* Options and what the benchmark keeps

~Solver::set_quickmin_options~ takes ~QuickMinOptions~. ~rgmin()~ is
the session above. ~eon()~ is eOn's ~Quickmin::step~: fixed ~Δt~, one
projection, and a capped step that leaves the velocity at its
uncapped value. ~per_atom()~ projects each xyz triple of the atomic
block on its own force, so one atom moving against its force stops
without stopping the others. ~curvature_cap = Some(c)~ bounds ~Δt~ by
~c / sqrt(λ̂)~, with ~λ̂ = |y| / |s|~ from the last step's force
difference.

On ~E = λ x² / 2~ with the projection inactive, the step is the
symplectic Euler map with characteristic polynomial
~r² - (2 - h) r + 1~, ~h = Δt² λ~. Its roots stay on the unit circle for
~0 ≤ h ≤ 4~, so ~Δt < 2 / sqrt(λ_max)~ is the stability limit. A step
from rest moves ~Δt² F~ and lowers the energy exactly when ~h < 2~;
with ~λ̂~ equal to the mode's curvature, ~c < sqrt(2)~ keeps that.
With a kept velocity ~v = c F~ the step length along ~F~ is
~Δt (c + Δt)~, and ~c~ grows while the walker stays on one side, so
the eOn step can raise the energy even under ~Δt² λ_max < 2~
(~validation/quickmin.py~). The projection itself never lengthens
~v~ and leaves no component along ~-F~
(~formal/RgminContracts/QuickMin.lean~); in binary64 it can lengthen
~v~ by at most ~B(n) - 1~ relative, 7 machine epsilons for ~n = 6~
(~validation/quickmin.sollya~).

~bench/atomistic~ (rgpot potentials, 5 starts each, per-atom cap 0.2,
~fmax < 0.01~), mean force calls and converged starts:

| options                    | LJ38          | Pt7 island | Al slab |
|----------------------------+---------------+------------+---------|
| ~eon()~                    | 2001 (0 of 5) |       18.6 |    24.6 |
| ~rgmin()~                  | 1344 (4 of 5) |       19.6 |    27.6 |
| ~per_atom()~               | 645 (5 of 5)  |       32.0 |    28.2 |
| ~rgmin()~, ~cap Some(0.7)~ | 1167 (5 of 5) |       20.0 |    27.6 |
| FIRE                       | 411           |       50.6 |    60.2 |
| FIRE 2.0 (Guénolé 2020)    | 871           |       26.0 |    33.8 |
| L-BFGS                     | 169           |       10.0 |    14.0 |

The eOn options never converge LJ38 from random packings in 2000
steps; the FIRE time step is what converges it. The per-atom
projection halves the LJ38 calls and costs 12 calls on the seven-atom
island, so it is an option, not the default. A restart or a backtrack
after an energy rise, a ten-fold ~Δt_max~, and dropping the clamp
rescale moved the LJ38 mean by at most 9 calls and the others by at
most 0.4, and none of them converged another start; they were removed.
