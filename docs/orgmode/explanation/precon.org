* Exp, Lindh, and the bonded metric

Three sparse symmetric positive definite matrices share one solve and
one rebuild rule. The solve is Jacobi-preconditioned conjugate
gradients. The matrix is held until the largest atomic move exceeds
half the nearest-neighbour distance.

** Exp

Packwood, Kermode, Mones, Bernstein, Woolley, Gould, Ortner, and
Csányi, J. Chem. Phys. 144, 164109 (2016),
https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4947024.

The caller supplies the neighbour list. The weight on a pair is
~exp(-A (r / r_nn - 1))~ with ~A = 3~. Off-diagonal blocks are
~-μ c_ij I_3~. The diagonal is ~μ (C_stab + sum_j c_ij) I_3~ with
~C_stab = 0.1~. ~r_nn~ is the largest per-atom nearest-neighbour
distance. ~μ~ is the Rayleigh quotient of a sine probe against this
matrix at ~μ = 1~. The probe's shifted gradient is an extra force
evaluation and is counted with the other force calls.

** Lindh

Lindh, Bernhardsson, Karlström, and Malmqvist, Chem. Phys. Lett. 241,
423 (1995), https://doi.org/10.1016/0009-2614(95)00646-L.

The weights come from the paper's element tables for H, B, C, N, O, F,
Si, P, S, and Cl. A metal or a noble gas is not in those tables. Pairs
inside the cutoff come from a cell list: bins in lexicographic order,
then the pairs sorted by atom index, so the matrix does not depend on
the thread count.

** Bonded metric

Makri, Ortner, and Kermode, J. Chem. Phys. 150, 094109 (2019),
https://doi.org/10.1063/1.5064465, when the curvatures are that
field's. ~bonded_terms~ with caller-supplied constants is the same
matrix and is a bonded geometric metric until those constants are the
field's. Harmonic bonds and angles, Morse bonds, and harmonic torsions
are the Rust terms. The C struct carries the harmonic bonds and angles.
Morse and the FF torsion stay in Rust. The Lindh torsion is a separate
flag.

** Which one

~select_precon~ reads the geometry and the atomic numbers.

| geometry | atoms | elements | field | choice |
|---+---:|---|---|---|
| bulk, surface | >= 100 | any | ignored | Exp |
| bulk, surface | < 100 | any | ignored | identity |
| molecule, cluster | any | Lindh set | no | Lindh |
| molecule, cluster | >= 100 | metal or noble gas | no | Exp |
| molecule, cluster | < 100 | metal or noble gas | no | identity |
| molecule, cluster | any | any | yes | bonded metric |

The 100-atom bulk threshold is the copper vacancy row. Cu147 is the
cluster of that size. Water is the Lindh molecule. Ar38 and Pt7 are
the identity clusters. The parallel kernels stay serial below ~N_PAR~
atoms. That threshold is an atom count, not a thread count, so the
result is the same at 1, 2, 3, and 8 threads.

** H0 and the metric

L-BFGS multiplies its compact Hessian approximation by an initial
scale. Installing ~P~ sets that scale to ~P^{-1}~, which is one
application of the solve. The two-loop recurrence then runs in the
Euclidean pair inner product, started from a Newton step in ~P~.

Nonlinear CG and FIRE need the inner product that defines conjugacy
and the velocity mix. Both use ~P~ itself: the search direction is
~-P^{-1} g~, and the FIRE velocity is mixed with that direction. The
same ~μ~ that scales ~P~ scales the direction, so the first trial step
along it is order 1. Opening the line search at the control step
(0.01 in the bench) spends several extrapolations climbing back to
that step. The opening trial is Nocedal-Wright equation 3.60, and 1
on the first iteration when a metric is installed. A rebuilt ~P~
restarts the recurrence at steepest descent because the stored
~P^{-1} g~ was formed with the old matrix.
