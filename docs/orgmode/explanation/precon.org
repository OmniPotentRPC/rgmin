* Exp, Lindh, and the bonded metric

Three sparse symmetric positive definite matrices share one solve and
one rebuild rule. The solve is Jacobi-preconditioned conjugate
gradients. The matrix is held until the largest atomic move exceeds
half the nearest-neighbour distance.

** Exp

Packwood, Kermode, Mones, Bernstein, Woolley, Gould, Ortner, and
Csányi, J. Chem. Phys. 144, 164109 (2016),
https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4947024.

The caller supplies the neighbour list. The weight on a pair is
~exp(-A (r / r_nn - 1))~ with ~A = 3~. Off-diagonal blocks are
~-μ c_ij I_3~. The diagonal is ~μ (C_stab + sum_j c_ij) I_3~ with
~C_stab = 0.1~. ~r_nn~ is the largest per-atom nearest-neighbour
distance. ~μ~ is the Rayleigh quotient of a sine probe against this
matrix at ~μ = 1~. The probe's shifted gradient is an extra force
evaluation and is counted with the other force calls. ASE's
~estimate_mu~ replaces a value below 1 with 1. rgmin keeps the
quotient. That cap is in eV/Å², so it is not invariant under a
change of energy or length unit. The pair weight and the sine probe
use one portable libm, so those bits match on x86_64 and aarch64.

** Lindh

Lindh, Bernhardsson, Karlström, and Malmqvist, Chem. Phys. Lett. 241,
423 (1995), https://doi.org/10.1016/0009-2614(95)00646-L.

The weights come from the paper's element tables for H, B, C, N, O, F,
Si, P, S, and Cl. A metal or a noble gas is not in those tables. Pairs
inside the cutoff come from a cell list: bins in lexicographic order,
then the pairs sorted by atom index, so the matrix does not depend on
the thread count.

** Bonded metric

Makri, Ortner, and Kermode, J. Chem. Phys. 150, 094109 (2019),
https://doi.org/10.1063/1.5064465, when the curvatures are that
field's. The matrix is the reduced (absolute-curvature) Hessian, which
matches ASE ~FF~ with ~hessian='reduced'~. ASE's default is
~spectral~. ~bonded_terms~ with caller-supplied constants is the same
matrix and is a bonded geometric metric until those constants are the
field's. Harmonic bonds and angles, Morse bonds, and harmonic torsions
are the Rust terms. The C struct carries the harmonic bonds and angles.
Morse and the FF torsion stay in Rust. The Lindh torsion is a separate
flag.

** Which one

~select_precon~ reads the geometry, the atomic numbers, and the
optimiser. ~RGMIN_PRECON_AUTO~ uses the session's own method, so C
does not pass the family.

| optimizer | geometry | atoms | elements | field | choice |
|---+---|---:|---|---|---|
| FIRE | any | any | any | any | identity |
| L-BFGS, CG | bulk, surface | >= 100 | any | ignored | Exp |
| L-BFGS, CG | bulk, surface | < 100 | any | ignored | identity |
| L-BFGS, CG | molecule, cluster | <= N_LINDH | Lindh set | no | Lindh |
| L-BFGS, CG | molecule, cluster | > N_LINDH | Lindh set | no | Exp at >= 100, else identity |
| L-BFGS, CG | molecule, cluster | any | Lindh set | yes | bonded metric |
| L-BFGS, CG | molecule, cluster | >= 100 | metal or noble gas | ignored | Exp |
| L-BFGS, CG | molecule, cluster | < 100 | metal or noble gas | ignored | identity |

The 100-atom line is ASE's ~PreconLBFGS~ rule. The bench has no bulk
below 100 atoms and no Cu147. The copper vacancy, 2047 atoms, is the
bulk row that uses Exp (L-BFGS 16 calls with none, 11 with Exp).
Water is the Lindh molecule (L-BFGS 5 calls, the same as none). Ar38
and Pt7 are the identity clusters. Lindh on lj38 ends at −164.88
against −167.77. The bonded metric on pt7 takes 18, 29, and 78 calls
against 10, 18, and 49, and on lj38 it ends at −166.65, so the field
is taken only for the covalent set.

~N_LINDH~ is 2,000. On bulk diamond Si with a 4 Å cutoff the Jacobi
PCG iteration counts are Lindh 69, 124, 140 and Exp 21, 48, 85 at
160, 2,000, and 32,000 atoms. Eight solves on one thread take Lindh
0.031 s, 0.914 s, and 34.9 s against Exp 0.001 s, 0.031 s, and 1.03 s.
The iteration count levels off near 140, and 2,000 atoms is the
largest measured size at which eight Lindh solves still finish in
about a second.

Each kernel has its own atom-count gate, measured as the smallest
size where two threads beat one by more than the run-to-run range.
Lindh apply and Lindh solve gate at 2,000 atoms. Exp apply and Exp
solve gate at 32,000 atoms (at 8,000 atoms the Exp gaps, 0.67 ms and
0.015 s, sit inside ranges of 0.96 ms and 0.059 s). Assembly stays
serial: at 100,000 atoms the Exp gap is 0.026 s inside a range of
0.027 s. The gate does not read the thread count, so the result is
the same at 1, 2, 3, and 8 threads. Peak RSS at 100,000 atoms is
2.65 GB.

** H0 and the metric

L-BFGS multiplies its compact Hessian approximation by an initial
scale. Installing ~P~ sets that scale to ~P^{-1}~, which is one
application of the solve. The two-loop recurrence then runs in the
Euclidean pair inner product, started from a Newton step in ~P~.

Nonlinear CG and FIRE need the inner product that defines conjugacy
and the velocity mix. Both use ~P~ itself: the search direction is
~-P^{-1} g~, and the FIRE velocity is mixed with that direction. The
same ~μ~ that scales ~P~ scales the direction, so the first trial step
along it is order 1. Opening the line search at the control step
(0.01 in the bench) spends several extrapolations climbing back to
that step. The opening trial is Nocedal-Wright equation 3.60, and 1
on the first iteration when a metric is installed. A rebuilt ~P~
restarts the recurrence at steepest descent because the stored
~P^{-1} g~ was formed with the old matrix.
