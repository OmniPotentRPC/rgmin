* Choose and install a preconditioner

~select_precon(kind, z, has_force_field)~ returns the metric. ~z~ is
one atomic number per free atom.

- Bulk or surface, at least 100 atoms: Exp. The copper vacancy bench
  is that row (L-BFGS 16 / 11 / 16 calls for none / exp / lindh).
  Below 100 atoms the same geometries stay at the identity.
- Molecule or cluster, and the caller has a force field: the bonded
  metric. Water with a field is that case.
- Molecule or cluster, every element in {H, B, C, N, O, F, Si, P, S, Cl}:
  Lindh. Water without a field is that case (L-BFGS, 5 calls, the same
  as the unpreconditioned run).
- Metal or noble-gas cluster of at least 100 atoms: Exp. Cu147 is that
  size. Below 100 atoms the cluster stays at the identity. Ar38 is
  that case: Lindh on lj38 stopped at −164.88 against −167.77, and CG
  and FIRE did not converge. Pt7 is the metal cluster below 100 atoms.

Install the choice with one call. L-BFGS stores ~P^{-1}~ as ~H_0~.
FIRE and nonlinear CG use ~P~ as the metric of the direction.

#+begin_src rust
use rgmin::{ExpParams, Neighbours, PreconSpec, Solver};

solver.set_precon(PreconSpec::Exp {
    positions: x.view(),
    neighbours: &neigh,
    params: &ExpParams::default(),
})?;
#+end_src

~set_exp~, ~set_lindh~, and ~set_ff~ are the same three arms.
~clear_precon~ drops the metric. ~precon_scale~ is the estimated ~μ~,
or ~None~ until the sine probe has run.

From C, fill ~rgmin_precon_t~ and call ~rgmin_solver_set_precon~.
~RGMIN_PRECON_AUTO~ uses the rule above. A nonzero bond or angle count
is the force field. Every index is checked against ~dim / 3~ in that
call. Morse bonds and FF torsions are Rust-only. The Lindh torsion
term is the ~dihedrals~ flag.

#+begin_src cpp
auto p = rgmin::Precon::lindh(numbers).cell(a, b, c);
solver.set_precon(p);
#+end_src

The neighbour list for Exp is the caller's. Lindh and the bonded
metric build their own pairs with a cell list. The bench's ~-ff~ arm
uses unit bond curvatures and an angle curvature of 0.1. That is a
bonded geometric metric, not the parameterised field of Makri, Ortner,
and Kermode 2019.
