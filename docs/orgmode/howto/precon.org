* Choose and install a preconditioner

~select_precon(kind, z, has_force_field, family)~ returns the metric.
~z~ is one atomic number per free atom. ~family~ is ~QuasiNewton~,
~Cg~, or ~Fire~. FIRE returns the identity. Exp and Lindh remain
available when the caller names them.

- Bulk or surface, at least 100 atoms, L-BFGS or CG: Exp. The 100-atom
  line is ASE's ~PreconLBFGS~ rule. The bench has no bulk below 100
  atoms. The copper vacancy, 2047 atoms, is the bulk row (L-BFGS 16
  calls with none, 11 with Exp). Below 100 atoms the same geometries
  stay at the identity.
- Molecule or cluster, a force field, and every element in the Lindh
  set: the bonded metric. Water with a field is that case. Pt7 with
  the geometric metric takes more calls than none, and lj38 with it
  ends at a different minimum, so a metal or a noble gas does not
  take the field.
- Molecule or cluster, every element in {H, B, C, N, O, F, Si, P, S, Cl},
  at most ~N_LINDH~ atoms: Lindh. Water without a field is that case
  (L-BFGS, 5 calls, the same as the unpreconditioned run).
- Metal or noble-gas cluster of at least 100 atoms: Exp. The 100-atom
  line is ASE's ~PreconLBFGS~ rule. The bench does not contain Cu147.
  Below 100 atoms the cluster stays at the identity. Ar38 is that case:
  Lindh on lj38 stopped at −164.88 against −167.77. Pt7 is the metal
  cluster below 100 atoms.

Install the choice with one call. L-BFGS stores ~P^{-1}~ as ~H_0~.
FIRE and nonlinear CG use ~P~ as the metric of the direction.

#+begin_src rust
use rgmin::{ExpParams, Neighbours, PreconSpec, Solver};

solver.set_precon(PreconSpec::Exp {
    positions: x.view(),
    neighbours: &neigh,
    params: &ExpParams::default(),
})?;
#+end_src

~set_exp~, ~set_lindh~, and ~set_ff~ are the same three arms.
~clear_precon~ drops the metric. ~precon_scale~ is the estimated ~μ~,
or ~None~ until the sine probe has run.

From C, fill ~rgmin_precon_t~ and call ~rgmin_solver_set_precon~.
~RGMIN_PRECON_AUTO~ uses the rule above and the session's method.
A nonzero bond or angle count is the force field, and it applies
only for a covalent molecule or cluster. Every index is checked
against ~dim / 3~ in that call. Neighbour and anchor indices are
~size_t~. Bond and angle indices are ~uint32_t~. Morse bonds and FF torsions are Rust-only. The Lindh torsion
term is the ~dihedrals~ flag.

#+begin_src cpp
auto p = rgmin::Precon::lindh(numbers).cell(a, b, c);
solver.set_precon(p);
#+end_src

The neighbour list for Exp is the caller's. Lindh and the bonded
metric build their own pairs with a cell list. The bench's ~-ff~ arm
uses unit bond curvatures and an angle curvature of 0.1. That is a
bonded geometric metric, not the parameterised field of Makri, Ortner,
and Kermode 2019.
